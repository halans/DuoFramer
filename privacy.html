Privacy Policy

Effective 22 November 2026

In short: DuoFramer doesn't collect any information about you. There's no account and no analytics. Your photos are framed on your iPhone and never uploaded. The one thing that leaves your device is a photo's location, sent to Apple Maps to look up its place name.

What DuoFramer accesses, and why

DuoFramer asks before it uses anything that needs your permission.

AccessWhat it's used for
Photos Showing your library in DuoFramer's photo browser so you can choose photos to frame, and reading each photo's camera details, date and location to fill in the frame. With limited access, DuoFramer only sees the photos you select. You can also skip this and use the system photo picker, which shares only the photos you pick.
Photos (add) Saving framed photos to your library, in a DuoFramer album, when you choose Save to Photos.

DuoFramer doesn't ask for your location. Place names come from the location already stored in a photo.

What leaves your device

To turn a photo's coordinates into a place name such as "Wānaka, New Zealand", DuoFramer sends those coordinates to Apple Maps through Apple's MapKit framework. Apple handles this request under the Apple Privacy Policy. No photo, name or account information is sent, and DuoFramer doesn't keep a record of the request. If a photo has no location, or you type your own place name, nothing is sent.

Nothing else leaves your device unless you share it yourself, for example by sharing a frame or a carousel from the share sheet.

On-device analysis

When a frame crops your photo, DuoFramer uses Apple's Vision framework to find faces, or the most eye-catching part of the photo, so they stay in view. This runs entirely on your iPhone. DuoFramer doesn't recognise who anyone is, and keeps no face data after choosing the crop.

What's stored on your iPhone

  • The frames you create, in DuoFramer's own storage, shown in My Frames. Delete any of them in the app, or delete the app to remove them all.
  • Your collections: their names and which frames are in them.
  • A short list of the photos DuoFramer has saved to your library, so its photo browser can hide them.
  • A few preferences, such as your appearance, favourite templates, slideshow timing and whether you've seen the welcome screen.

Like other app data, this may be included in your iPhone's backups, such as an iCloud Backup, under the backup settings you choose.

What's in your frames

A frame shows only the details you choose to show: the camera, settings, date, place, coordinates or weather. Each exported image carries a small "DuoFramer" tag and keyword in its metadata, so DuoFramer can recognise its own frames and photo apps can find them. The original photo's camera data and GPS location aren't copied into the exported file.

What DuoFramer doesn't do

  • It doesn't upload your photos anywhere.
  • It doesn't include analytics, advertising or tracking, or any third-party code that does.
  • It doesn't ask you to create an account or give your name or email address.
  • It doesn't sell or share data, because it doesn't collect any.

Your choices

You can change DuoFramer's Photos access at any time in the Settings app, under Apps → DuoFramer → Photos. Choose Full Access to browse your whole library, Limited Access to choose specific photos, or None to use only the system photo picker.

Children

DuoFramer doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If DuoFramer's handling of your information changes, this page will be updated and the effective date above will change.
